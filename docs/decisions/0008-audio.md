# 0008 — Background score: generated audio, provenance and playback

Status: accepted · 2026-08-23 · supersedes nothing · relates to
[0004 hosting](0004-hosting.md), [0007 imagery](0007-imagery.md)

## Context

The campaign is read, not watched. A visitor spends twenty minutes moving a
clock across four months of 1914, and the room they read it in is silent.
A score can carry what the prose cannot — that the plan is a machine, that
the retreat does not end, that the trench line is not a conclusion — but
background music on a history site fails in three well-known ways: it
autoplays, it is too loud to read over, and it turns a casualty figure into
entertainment.

We also have to answer for where the music came from. Every photograph in
this project carries an archive record, a licence and a note distinguishing
what is documented from what is inferred ([0007](0007-imagery.md)). Music
generated by a model needs the same discipline for different questions.

## Decision

**A score of cues, in the shared registry.** One manifest per cue at
`content/shared/audio/<slug>/cue.json`, holding the `Cue` entity. Cues are
shared rather than pack-scoped: a score written for 1914 can underscore
another era, and _which_ cue plays _when_ is the pack's business, not the
asset's. Audio gets its own entity rather than being bent into `Media` —
it has no width or height, and it does have a key, a tempo and a loop point.

**Masters stay local; derivatives go to the bucket.** Exactly the
arrangement [0004](0004-hosting.md) sets for images. The WAV beside the
manifest is a staging copy, git-ignored; `npm run audio` writes Opus 96k and
AAC 128k into a git-ignored `.derived/`, and `--upload` syncs only those to
`/assets/audio/`. A WAV master is tens of megabytes and nothing in a browser
wants one.

**Loudness is matched, not compressed.** Every cue is normalised to
**−20 LUFS** integrated with two-pass EBU R128 `loudnorm` and `linear=true`,
so the correction is a single gain change and the music keeps its dynamics.
−20 is well below streaming's −14: this is music to read over. Generated
masters arrive at streaming loudness — the first seven came in between −13.2
and −16.4 LUFS, a 3.2 LU spread — so passing them through unchanged would be
both too loud and audibly uneven at every crossfade. A per-cue `mixDb` trims
a bed below the cue it joins.

**Provenance is required.** `provenance` carries the tool, the model, the
prompt, the exclude list, the date and the licence, and a cue without them
does not ship — `scripts/check-content.sh` and the validator both refuse a
manifest whose provenance is a placeholder, on the same bar the imagery
policy sets. The prompt matters most: a generated asset that cannot be
regenerated or defended is not documented, it is merely present.

**Playback rules, binding on whatever consumes the index.**

- **Never autoplay.** Off by default, one obvious control, and the choice is
  remembered. Browsers block autoplay anyway; a history site that starts
  playing music unasked reads as a toy.
- **Crossfade on a stage change, never restart.** Scrubbing across three
  stages should slide, not stutter.
- **Nothing is signalled by audio alone.** Anything the score conveys must
  also be visible, or it does not exist for the great majority of readers,
  who have sound off.
- **Silence is a cue.** On 22 August 1914 — the bloodiest day in French
  history, which has its own beat — the music stops. The pack already
  refuses to gamify that day; silence after an hour of sound is the strongest
  treatment available and costs nothing.

## Consequences

Adding a cue means adding a manifest and running `npm run audio`; CI checks
the index covers every manifest but does not transcode, so ffmpeg is a
contributor's tool rather than a build dependency. Loudness matching means a
regenerated cue slots in without remixing the rest. Requiring the prompt in
the manifest means the score is reproducible: a cue can be regenerated,
extended or replaced years from now with the same brief.

The licence is only as durable as the plan it was generated under. The first
seven cues were made on a Suno Pro plan (model v5.5), which grants commercial
use for songs made while subscribed; that is recorded per cue rather than
assumed, because the terms attach to the moment of generation.
