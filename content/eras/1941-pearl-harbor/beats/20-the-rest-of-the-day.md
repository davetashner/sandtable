*Almost simultaneously the Japanese attacked Hawaii, the Philippines, Wake, Guam, Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaya and Thailand.*[^morton-1962] That sentence is the reason this chapter exists. Pearl Harbor is what the day is called in one country; it was one of eight or nine places attacked inside about twenty-four hours, and it was not the first.

Advance units of the 25th Army had left Hainan on 4 December, and **on the morning of the 8th** — which is still the 7th east of the date line — they landed at [Singora](place:singora) and [Patani](place:patani) in southern Thailand and at [Kota Bharu](place:kota-bharu) across the Malayan border. The Thai landings were unopposed and were helped by the local authorities. The Kota Bharu force met British aircraft and beach guns, pulled back with heavy losses, and came in again later in the day under stronger air cover.[^morton-1962] No source this pack could open gives an hour for any of it, so the event on the timeline is drawn as the whole morning rather than as a minute.

At 0545 on Guam the naval governor was told by the Commander in Chief of the Asiatic Fleet that Hawaii was under attack; in less than three hours bombers from Saipan were over the island.[^hough-ludwig-shaw-1958] On [Wake](place:wake-island), an uncoded transmission from Oahu came through at 0650, Morning Colors was sounded at 0800 while *Arizona* was burning on the far side of the ocean, and at 1150 a battery commander at Peacock Point saw aircraft coming and reached for the telephone. By 1210 the strike was over.[^hough-ludwig-shaw-1958]

The worst of it, for the Americans, was the Philippines — and it was the worst because it was not a surprise. A commercial radio station on Luzon picked up the news of Pearl Harbor *shortly after 0300 (0830 in Hawaii)*.[^craven-cate-aaf-1] Nine hours later, *at 1215 the 20th Pursuit Squadron at Clark, whose planes had just completed refueling, made ready to take off. At that moment the first formation of Japanese bombers appeared over Clark Field.*[^morton-1953] [Iba](place:iba-field) was hit at the same time, its fighters circling to land and low on fuel. By the end of the day only 17 of the Far East Air Force's 35 B-17s remained, and 53 P-40s and 3 P-35s had been destroyed.[^morton-1953]

[Hong Kong](place:hong-kong) lost the airfield at Kai Tak to aircraft from Canton on the morning of the 8th and surrendered on Christmas Day.[^morton-1962] Guam had no coastal guns and no aircraft: four hundred Japanese sailors came ashore north of [Agana](place:agana) behind flares at four in the morning on the 10th, and the island was given up shortly after six.[^hough-ludwig-shaw-1958] The same day, off [Kuantan](place:kuantan), [*Prince of Wales* and *Repulse*](1941-pearl-harbor:force-z) — sent out from Singapore without air cover to break up the landings — were sunk by land-based naval aircraft.[^morton-1962]

Read the harbour on its own and 7 December is an outrage done to one country. Read it with this map open and it is the first morning of a plan: a near-simultaneous move against every base between Hawaii and the Malay Barrier, executed by a navy that believed it had six months.
