Tokyo had told its embassy to deliver the memorandum at one o'clock in the afternoon, Washington time. One o'clock in Washington was half past seven in Honolulu: twenty-five minutes before the first bomb.

The embassy did not manage it. The Secretary of State's own memorandum of the conversation records the sequence with a bureaucratic flatness that is more damning than any adjective: the appointment asked for at 1:00, put back to 1:45, the ambassador arriving at 2:05, received at 2:20. [Nomura](person:nomura-kichisaburo) explained that he had been instructed to deliver it at one and that decoding had taken longer than expected; asked why one o'clock, he said he did not know, that was his instruction.[^peace-and-war-1943]

Meanwhile, at 0840 in Honolulu — 1410 in Washington, ten minutes before the two envoys were shown in — level and dive bombers renewed the attack on Oahu.[^craven-cate-aaf-1]

[Hull](person:hull-cordell) read several pages, asked whether the document was presented on instructions from the Japanese government, was told that it was, and read the rest. The [memorandum](1941-pearl-harbor:document-japanese-memorandum) ends by regretting *to have to notify hereby the American Government that in view of the attitude of the American Government it cannot but consider that it is impossible to reach an agreement through further negotiations*.[^peace-and-war-1943] It breaks off a negotiation. It does not declare war and it gives no warning of anything.

Then Hull said this, and it is on the record because the department wrote it down that afternoon:

> I must say that in all my conversations with you during the last nine months I have never uttered one word of untruth. This is borne out absolutely by the record. In all my fifty years of public service I have never seen a document that was more crowded with infamous falsehoods and distortions—infamous falsehoods and distortions on a scale so huge that I never imagined until today that any Government on this planet was capable of uttering them.[^peace-and-war-1943]

The two envoys left without saying anything.

By ten o'clock in Honolulu the raid was over and the aircraft were heading north.[^morton-1962] In under two hours eight battleships, three light cruisers and three destroyers had been sunk or put out of action; 92 naval aircraft destroyed and 31 damaged; 96 Army aircraft lost; 2,403 dead and 1,178 wounded, most of them naval. The Japanese had lost about fifty aircraft and five midget submarines.[^morton-1962]

The day is almost always counted in ships. It is worth counting it once in the people standing on them: one of the eight battleships was *West Virginia*, and [Doris Miller](person:miller-doris) was a mess attendant on her — the rating the Navy kept for Black sailors, who were not permitted to hold a gunnery rate. His card carries what he did that morning and what this pack could and could not read about it.
