The pack ends on open water.

When _Hiryū_ went down she took most of her engineering force with her — but not all of it. The Combined Fleet record of her movements counts seventy men marooned aboard, of whom "only thirty-five are eventually rescued by the U.S. Navy, including her Chief Engineer."[^combinedfleet] Those thirty-five got away in one of her boats, and for two weeks the ocean held them.

The Naval Historical Center tells the American half: "On 14 June, a PBY spotted a small boat hundreds of miles away from Midway. It was seen again on the 19th, and the seaplane tender Ballard was directed to the area. There she found thirty-five Japanese navymen, members of carrier Hiryu's engineering force who were left for dead when she was abandoned before dawn on 5 June."[^nhc-midway-rescue]

Among _Ballard_'s radio gang was [Jay Tashner](person:tashner-john-charles-jr) — twenty-one now, the Patrol Wing TWO radioman of 7 December — whose service record credits him with participating in the capture: "36 Japanese officers and men", by the sheet's count, against the Center's thirty-five, a difference left standing as written.[^tashner-service-record] The Navy's photographers met the boat's passengers twice. Above, they sit exhausted on mats on _Ballard_'s deck; the next day they were brought ashore under guard onto the coral of [Midway](place:midway), to be prepared for transport to Hawaii.[^nhc-midway-rescue]

The sailor who had spent the week after [Pearl Harbor](place:pearl-harbor) recovering the dead from the oil spent 19 June helping take the enemy's living out of the sea. The pack stops there.
