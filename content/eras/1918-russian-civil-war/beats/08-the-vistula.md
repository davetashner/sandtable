The Polish–Soviet war is the one campaign in this pack that a reader of the 1914
packs would recognise, and it still is not a front: it is two armies chasing
each other across seven hundred kilometres in six weeks, twice, in opposite
directions.

[Piłsudski](person:pilsudski-jozef) went first. Polish forces in alliance with
Petliura's Ukrainian nationalists entered [Kiev](place:kiev) on 6 May. They held
it for five weeks: [Budyonny](person:budyonny-semyon)'s cavalry got into their
rear at Berdichev and Zhitomir on 8 June and the Red Army was back in the city
on the 12th.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

Then the pursuit did not stop.
[Tukhachevsky](person:tukhachevsky-mikhail), commanding the Western Front at
twenty-seven from a headquarters at [Smolensk](place:smolensk), took
[Minsk](place:minsk) on 11 July, Vilna on the 14th and
[Brest-Litovsk](place:brest-litovsk) on 1 August, and on 31 July a Polish
Revolutionary Committee of Polish Communists was installed at Białystok to be
the government of the Soviet Poland that was about to
exist.[^chamberlin-1935-2] Behind Poland was Germany. This is the fortnight in
which the Russian revolution was a European project with an address.

What happened next is the subject of the
[chapter](1918-russian-civil-war:warsaw-1920): Polish forces south of Warsaw
attacked north into the flank of an army group that had gone past the city, and
by 21 August Brest-Litovsk was Polish again and the Red Army was in general
retreat from the Vistula.[^chamberlin-1935-2] Peace talks opened at Riga on
21 September; the treaty of 18 March 1921 fixed a frontier a long way east of
where anyone had expected and left Poland's Ukrainian allies on the wrong side
of it. It lasted until September 1939.

Two things about this campaign to keep hold of.

**It was decided by supply and by distance, which is the pack's argument
again.** Seven hundred kilometres in six weeks is a little under half a
kilometre an hour of straight-line displacement — glacial by the standards of
1944 and astonishing by the standards of this war — and an army that has done it
across a country with no railways it can use arrives at a great river with its
ammunition, its cavalry and its right-hand neighbour a long way behind it.

**And it produced the quarrel that killed people seventeen years later.** The
1st Cavalry Army was in the south, near Lvov, and did not turn north. Whether it
was ordered to and refused, and whose fault that was — Tukhachevsky's for the
plan, Budyonny's for the disobedience, Stalin's as the political member of the
council of the South-Western Front — became one of the permanent arguments of
Soviet military history. In 1937 the men on one side of it shot the men on the
other.
