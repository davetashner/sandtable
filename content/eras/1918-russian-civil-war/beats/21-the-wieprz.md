**This chapter has no routes, and the reason is worth stating rather than
hiding.** The Vistula in August 1920 is the one week of the war where a zoom-in
with formations and hourly paths would tell a reader something a paragraph
cannot — and the pack could not reach a source that puts divisions on the ground
at that resolution. Drawing them anyway, from the general shape of the operation,
would produce a map that looked like evidence. So what is here is the shape, in
words, with markers.

The shape is this. [Tukhachevsky](person:tukhachevsky-mikhail)'s Western Front
had come seven hundred kilometres in six weeks and was strung out across the
approaches to [Warsaw](place:warsaw), with its supply behind it, its
communications behind that, and its neighbouring front — the South-Western,
with the 1st Cavalry Army in it — away to the south around Lvov and pursuing an
objective of its own. Its main weight was north-west of the city, going for the
lower Vistula and the crossings, on the assumption that Warsaw would fall to
envelopment rather than assault.

That left, south-east of the city, a gap with a river in it — the Wieprz — and
almost nothing across it.

[Piłsudski](person:pilsudski-jozef) assembled a strike group there and sent it
north into the flank on 15 August (the date in the source this pack read; most
later accounts give the 16th). What it hit was not a line but the space between
two Soviet armies, and once it was through, the whole northern group was in the
air. [Brest-Litovsk](place:brest-litovsk) was back in Polish hands on 21 August
and the retreat from the Vistula was general.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

It is called the Miracle of the Vistula, which is a phrase from a Polish
political argument about who deserved the credit — the commander-in-chief who
was also the head of state, or the Providence his clerical opponents preferred
to thank. Neither party to that argument was much interested in the third
explanation, which is that an army five weeks past its railheads in a country
whose railways it did not control was not going to be able to react to anything.

What was at stake was not Poland. A Polish Revolutionary Committee of Polish
Communists had been sitting at Białystok since 31 July waiting to govern, and
what lay beyond Poland was Germany, where a revolution was still, in 1920,
thought to be a live possibility. This is the week in which the Russian
revolution stopped being a thing that was going to spread and became a thing
that was going to have to be built in one country. The consequences of that
sentence are the next fifteen years of Soviet history.
