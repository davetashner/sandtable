Three White armies came at Moscow and Petrograd in 1919 and none of them ever
came within eight hundred kilometres of another. That sentence is the year.

[Kolchak](person:kolchak-alexander) took Ufa on 13 March and drove west towards
the Volga; the offensive was stopped short of it by defeats around Buzuluk and
Buguruslan on 26 April; by 9 June the Reds had Ufa back and by 1 July
[Perm](place:perm), the northern shoulder of the whole front. In the south
[Denikin](person:denikin-anton) broke the Red front near Yuzovka on 19 May and
had Kharkov, Tsaritsyn and Ekaterinoslav inside six weeks. In the north-west
[Yudenich](person:yudenich-nikolai) started for Petrograd on 11 October and
reached the [Pulkovo heights](place:pulkovo) — sixteen kilometres from the
centre, close enough to see the city — before being pushed back on the 22nd and
over the frontier at [Narva](place:narva) in November.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

By the time the last of them moved, the first had already lost.

## Five hundred miles behind the line

In the middle of it comes the demonstration piece. On 10 August a Don Cossack
general named [Mamontov](person:mamontov-konstantin) went through the Red
Southern Front near Novokhopersk with seven or eight thousand picked cavalry.
Chamberlin explains why with an aside that is the whole geography of this pack
in one clause: "the lightly held and shifting fronts of the Russian civil war
were rather easy to 'break through'".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

He held Tambov from the 18th to the 21st, took Kozlov and Yelets and Ranenburg,
destroyed railway and telegraph as he went, fought the [Voronezh](place:voronezh) garrison for
several days, and rejoined the White lines on 19 September "after forty days of
wild raiding, in the course of which he covered a distance of about five hundred
miles".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

And it achieved nothing. His Cossacks would not stop plundering, which
disorganised his own corps faster than the riding did — the temptation to slip
away home with the loot "was irresistible" — and it made it impossible to raise
the countryside behind the Red front. Tambov province was not fond of the
Communists; it would be in open rebellion against them within a year. But, in
Chamberlin's flat sentence, "a White General could not arouse them against the
Soviets."[^chamberlin-1935-2]

## Orel

The Volunteer Army took [Orel](place:orel) on 13 October — the day after
Chernigov — with two hundred and fifty miles between it and Moscow and one large
town, [Tula](place:tula), where the rifles were made, in
between.[^chamberlin-1935-2] It is the closest the Whites came in the entire
war, and by every account the more thoughtful officers already knew it was over.

The front from the Volga to the Romanian frontier was held by nobody: the final
advances had been made by moving units from one part of it to another, "because
there were no more free reserves". At the same moment towns in southern Ukraine
were falling to [Makhno](person:makhno-nestor)'s insurgents, Denikin's own
headquarters at Taganrog was threatened, and an insurrection in Daghestan was
swallowing troops that were wanted at Tula. Chamberlin's judgement is that the
capture of Moscow had by then become "not so much a serious military objective
as a romantic dream, a means of escaping from the social and economic problems
which Denikin's regime was unable to solve".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

Orel was retaken on 20 October. Omsk fell on 14 November. From there it is
downhill in both directions for four months.

_(A note on the date. Chamberlin's narrative dates the fall of Orel to 13
October and his own chronological table to the 14th. This pack stores the
narrative date and keeps the disagreement on the event rather than deciding
it.)_
