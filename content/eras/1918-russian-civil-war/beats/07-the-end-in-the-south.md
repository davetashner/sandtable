Two endings, four thousand kilometres apart, five weeks apart.

The [Czechoslovak Legion](1918-russian-civil-war:formation-czechoslovak-legion),
which had begun the war by refusing to be disarmed and was now buying its
passage east along a railway it still controlled, handed
[Kolchak](person:kolchak-alexander) over to the Political Centre at
[Irkutsk](place:irkutsk) on 15 January 1920. A revolutionary committee took
over three days later with authorisation to execute him at its discretion. He
was shot on a hill outside the town on the morning of 7 February, together with
his prime minister Pepelyaev, and both bodies were put through a hole in the ice
of the Angara. He knew what was coming and, by general testimony, "bore himself
with courage and dignity throughout the period of his
imprisonment".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

What went east behind him was worse than an army in retreat. The Siberian
educated and middle classes, government officials, officers and their families
and everyone who had already fled Bolshevism once now had to move again, and
were "rapidly thinned by disease, hunger and cold".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

In the south the Red Army took [Rostov](place:rostov-on-don) on 8 January and
[Novorossiysk](place:novorossiysk) on 27 March. What could be got aboard a ship
went to the Crimea; what could not was left on the quay. The Kuban capital,
[Ekaterinodar](place:ekaterinodar), where Kornilov had been killed by a shell in
April 1918 and where the Volunteer Army had begun, went on 17 March.
[Denikin](person:denikin-anton) resigned on 4 April, nominated
[Wrangel](person:wrangel-pyotr) to succeed him, and left Russia on a British
warship. He lived another twenty-seven years and refused, in the next war, to
have anything to do with the Germans.

There was one White army left, on a peninsula, with a seven-kilometre isthmus
behind it.
