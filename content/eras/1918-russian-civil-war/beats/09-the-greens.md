The Greens are not a third army. They are most of the country, declining.

The overwhelming majority of the people in the territory this war was fought
over were peasants, and what they had got out of 1917 was the land — taken from the
landlords, parish by parish, in the autumn of that year. What they wanted after
that was to be left alone to farm it. Both of the sides with capital letters
wanted something else: the Reds wanted the grain, and took it with armed
detachments from June 1918 onwards; the Whites, whatever their generals said
about deferring the land question to a future assembly, arrived in the company
of the men who had owned the land in 1916.

So the villages fought both, and the shape of the war in the space between the
railways is a hundred small risings.

In eastern Ukraine the risings had a name and an idea.
[Makhno](person:makhno-nestor), a peasant from
[Gulyai-Polye](place:gulyai-polye) who had been sentenced to death at
twenty-two as an anarchist and reprieved to hard labour, fought the
Austro-German occupation, the Hetman, Petliura, Denikin, Wrangel and the
Bolsheviks — twice in formal alliance with the Red Army and twice destroyed by
it when the alliance had served. In November 1919, at the exact moment the White
front stood at Orel, he was taking towns in Denikin's rear and pulling troops
off the road to Moscow.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

That is where Chamberlin's sharpest sentence about the whole war sits. The three
most formidable enemies of Soviet power in the south were Denikin, Wrangel and
Makhno; and Makhno fought the other two harder than he fought the Communists,
because "between the conservative Generals and the peasant anarchist there could
be no basis of cooperation".[^chamberlin-1935-2] An anti-Bolshevik coalition was
not merely absent. It was impossible.

## Tambov

In August 1920, with the Whites down to one peninsula, the province that had
been the stormiest in the agrarian revolution of 1917 rose against the
requisitioning under a former Socialist Revolutionary and district police chief
named [Antonov](person:antonov-alexander). At its height, on the estimate of a
Chekist who fought them, some twenty thousand insurgents were under arms — and
Chamberlin adds the caveat that makes every such figure soft: "some of Antonov's
followers were 'bandits', as the Communists liked to call peasant rebels against
their requisitions, one day and peaceful farmers the next".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

The songs the insurgents sang were about requisitioning and conscription and
nothing else. One of them, in Chamberlin's literal translation: "Deserter I was
born, deserter I shall die. Shoot me on the spot; I don't go into the Red Army.
To us came a commissar and two Red Army soldiers. All the same we won't go.
Don't hope for us."[^chamberlin-1935-2]

The suppression, given to [Tukhachevsky](person:tukhachevsky-mikhail) in 1921,
used hostages, deportation and camps. By the end of July 1921 those camps held
about fifty thousand people, the majority of them women, the elderly and
children; typhus, cholera and hunger were in them, and in the autumn of 1921
mortality reached fifteen to twenty per cent a month.[^werth-2008]

## Why this decided it

Chamberlin's answer to the whole war runs through this beat. Both sides had
hostile peasantries; the difference was one of degree and of timing. "One year
of Kolchak's rule was sufficient to turn Siberia into a hornets' nest of
rebellious peasant partisans, who did half the Red Army's work for it." Denikin
got a shorter period and the same result. But at the moments when a general rising
in the villages would have destroyed the Bolsheviks — Kolchak approaching the
Volga, Denikin at Orel, Yudenich at Pulkovo — "the Red rear remained
firm".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

_(A note on this beat's window. The Green war ran from 1918 to 1922 without a
