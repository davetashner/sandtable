Germany's revolution on 9 November did two things at once. It let the Soviet
government annul Brest-Litovsk on the 13th, and it took the reason for the
Allied intervention away — as Mawdsley puts it, victory in the west "eliminated
the main incentive and military potential for large-scale involvement in
Russia". The German army went home over the winter and everything Brest-Litovsk
had signed away became a vacuum with several armies walking into it: the Red
Army took Minsk in December, Riga and Kharkov in January, Kiev in February.

Five days after the annulment, the anti-Bolshevik movement decided what it was
going to be.

At [Omsk](place:omsk) on the night of 17–18 November the Socialist
Revolutionary members of the Directory — the government that the Ufa conference
had put together in September to unify the eastern anti-Bolsheviks — were
arrested. The cabinet met in the morning. Nobody proposed releasing them.
"There was some half-hearted talk of carrying on the administration with a
Directory of three… But this was soon dropped; and there was general agreement
that a personal dictatorship offered the only solution for the political
crisis. Only one Minister, Shumilovsky, expressed dissent."[^chamberlin-1935-2]
The question then was who, and the cabinet chose
[Admiral Kolchak](person:kolchak-alexander), who accepted with the title of
Supreme Ruler and took the office of commander-in-chief of all Russian land and
naval forces as well.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

That is the moment the democratic counter-revolution ended. It had lasted from
8 June to 18 November, and its epitaph is Chamberlin's: the relatively
democratic bodies of the civil war — the Samara government, the Ufa Directory,
the Kuban Rada, the Ukrainian nationalists — "were pitifully weak and were
brushed aside by Reds and Whites with little difficulty".[^chamberlin-1935-2]
A group of Socialist Revolutionaries took to calling themselves _Ninisti_, from
the slogan "neither Lenin nor Kolchak"; the answer history gave them, in
Chamberlin's phrase, was "either Lenin or Kolchak".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

It is also the moment the White movement acquired the thing it could never put
down: a politics that had nothing to offer anybody. Sumpf's summary of what
would beat them is short — what they lacked to win was "the unity provided by an
ideology, an uncontested leader and a clearly defined project for a multiethnic
nation in revolution".[^sumpf-2014] They had just acquired the second of those
and destroyed any chance of the first and third in the same night.

Whether that is really why they lost is argued about, and the argument has a
card: [Did the Reds win, or did the Whites
lose?](1918-russian-civil-war:historiography-why-the-whites-lost)
