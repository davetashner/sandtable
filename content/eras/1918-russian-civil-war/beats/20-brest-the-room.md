There are no routes in this chapter and there is nothing to draw but a fortress
on a river, because nothing manoeuvred. What happened at
[Brest-Litovsk](place:brest-litovsk) happened in a room.

The Bolsheviks had promised an immediate peace without annexations or
indemnities. The Germans, who held the room, wanted annexations, and had the
Ukrainian Rada's own delegation at the table to make the loss of Ukraine look
like self-determination rather than conquest. Between those two positions
[Trotsky](person:trotsky-leon) spent eight weeks talking, because the party's
actual policy — never quite stated as one — was to wait for a revolution in
Berlin.

On 10 February he tried the formula that has his name on it: neither war nor
peace. No signature, no annexations accepted, and no fighting either; the war
was simply declared over unilaterally and the delegation went home. It is the
only genuinely novel move in the history of armistice diplomacy and it lasted
eight days. On 18 February the German army resumed its advance and found that
there was nothing in front of it at all. Within a week it had taken more ground
than in the previous year, and the argument inside the Bolshevik leadership —
Lenin for signing, Bukharin for a revolutionary war, Trotsky in the middle —
ended not because anyone was persuaded but because the alternative had been
demonstrated.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

New German terms arrived on 22 February, worse than the ones refused. The
Council of People's Commissars and the party's Central Committee agreed to sign
on the 23rd. The treaty was signed on 3 March and ratified by the Fourth
Congress of Soviets on the 15th, at which point the Left Socialist
Revolutionaries walked out of the government in protest. Four months later they
shot the German ambassador in Moscow, which is what a protest looks like when
the party making it has decided to become an insurrection.

## Why the frontier is not on the map

Article III of the treaty does not describe a line. It says that the territories
west of a line "determined by the contracting powers" — traced on a map
"submitted as an essential part of this treaty of peace" — are no longer under
Russian sovereignty, and leaves the exact fixing of it to a Russo-German
commission.[^avalon-brest-litovsk-1918]

That map is not reproduced with the text this project was able to read. So this
chapter shows the place where the line was agreed and does not draw the line.
The alternative — inventing a plausible frontier from memory and letting the map
assert it — is exactly the kind of thing a map does silently and a footnote
cannot undo.

What the treaty took, in the end, was about a third of the population of
European Russia, fifty-odd million people, most of the empire's coal and iron
and much of its grain. And it was annulled by the same government eight months
later, on 13 November 1918, four days after the revolution it had been waiting
for finally arrived — in Germany, too late to have prevented anything.
