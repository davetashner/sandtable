There were something like thirty-five thousand Czechs and Slovaks in Russia in
the spring of 1918 — prisoners of war and deserters from the Austro-Hungarian
army who had changed sides, "citizens of a state which in 1918 still existed
only in the imagination of its nationalist leaders".[^chamberlin-1935-2] With
the Eastern Front abolished by Brest-Litovsk they had agreed to go home to fight
in France, and the shortest way to France from [Penza](place:penza) was east: nine
thousand kilometres of the Trans-Siberian, then
[Vladivostok](place:vladivostok), then the Pacific, then
America, then the Atlantic.

They never got moving properly, and by May the
[Legion](1918-russian-civil-war:formation-czechoslovak-legion) was strung out in
six separate groups with more than five thousand miles between the westernmost
at Penza and the easternmost at Vladivostok: about fourteen thousand around
Vladivostok, eight thousand at Penza, a rather larger detachment at
[Chelyabinsk](place:chelyabinsk), and three small parties of two thousand, one
thousand and eight hundred scattered across central Siberia.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

On 14 May a Hungarian prisoner threw a piece of iron at a Czech soldier on the
platform at Chelyabinsk and was killed for it. The local Soviet arrested some
Czechs; their comrades walked into town with rifles and took them back. Then
Moscow, three days' unreliable telegraph away, decided to be firm. On 25 May
Trotsky wired every Soviet on the line that all Czechs were to be disarmed and
that "every Czech who is found armed on the railroad is to be shot on the
spot".[^chamberlin-1935-2]

No Soviet along that line had the means to do any such thing. What the order did
was inform thirty-five thousand armed men, sitting on the only railway in
Siberia, that their choice was to fight or be shot — and one town after another
went "with amazing rapidity": [Novo-Nikolaevsk](place:novonikolaevsk) and
Chelyabinsk on the 26th, Penza on the 28th, Tomsk on the 31st, Omsk on 7 June, Samara on the
8th.[^chamberlin-1935-2]

## What a railway is for

This is the point at which the Western Front's mental model stops being useful
and should be put down. Nothing here has a front. A corps holds five thousand
miles of country by sitting on the only line that crosses it, and the towns fall
in the order the timetable would take you through them. The unit of control is
the junction. Everything between the junctions belongs to whoever lives there,
which over the next three years turns out to matter more than any of it.

At Samara the Socialist Revolutionary members of the Constituent Assembly that
the Bolsheviks had dissolved in January came out of hiding and formed a
government. For five months there was a third Russia — parliamentary,
Socialist-Revolutionary, sheltering behind Czech bayonets — and it is the road
not taken in this war. It lasted until November.

The other thing that began that summer was quieter and mattered more. On
11 June the state started organising committees of the village poor and sending
armed detachments out from the towns to take grain, because its money was
worthless and it had nothing to sell.[^chamberlin-1935-2] Every Green rising in
this pack starts there.

Meanwhile the Allies were doing rather less than the phrase "fourteen foreign
armies" suggests. A small mixed force under British command had been sitting at
[Murmansk](place:murmansk) since early in the year, guarding the Allied military
stores that had piled up there, and Chamberlin is careful about what that was:
"so long as this force was defensive in character the Soviet Government had no
objection to its presence". It only turned aggressive at the end of June, when it
concluded a separate agreement with the Murmansk Soviet, "which was not loyal to
Moscow", and pushed its occupation south to Kem.[^chamberlin-1935-2] Archangel
was occupied on 2 August. Mawdsley, who wrote the standard history of the
intervention, sums the whole of 1918 up in a sentence:
direct action by British, French, American and Japanese forces "was confined to
occupation of remote ports in the northern parts of European Russia and of
Vladivostok on the Pacific".[^mawdsley-2014]
