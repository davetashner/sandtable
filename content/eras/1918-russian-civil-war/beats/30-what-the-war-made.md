This chapter is outside the campaign and keeps its own clock. It exists so the
forward causal links have somewhere real to point, and it makes one argument.

Holquist's version of it is the most economical: what was distinctive about the
Bolshevik regime "was less remarkable for the measures it took during the
extended period of war, revolution, and civil wars, than for the fact that it
continued these measures after that period had wound to an
end".[^holquist-2003] Every state in Europe learned things between 1914 and
1921 about how to move populations, count them, ration them, deport them and
shoot them. Most of them put the tools down afterwards.

Three of the things that did not get put down are in this pack.

**The grain.** Chamberlin, writing from Moscow in 1935 with collectivisation
going on around him and looking back at 1921, does not present the New Economic
Policy as a change of heart. With a ruined industry and an exhausted country,
"the Soviet leaders could not in 1921 inaugurate the policy which they launched
in 1929: forcible collectivization of the peasant households on a basis of
tractors and other large farm machines. There was nothing to do but to retreat
before the small peasant proprietor."[^chamberlin-1935-2] A retreat is a thing
you come back from, and in 1929 they came back — with the same vocabulary, the
same legal category of the kulak as an enemy, and the same assumption that the
countryside is a rear area to be requisitioned rather than a market to be bought
from.

**The man.** [Stalin](person:stalin-joseph)'s political education was this war
and it happened at Tsaritsyn, where he was sent in 1918 to supervise the
collection of grain and took over the military command instead. Chamberlin
records it in one line, before the legend had fully set: "Stalin, sent to the
Southeast to supervise the collection of grain, was a prominent figure in the
defense of Tsaritsin (which was subsequently renamed
Stalingrad)".[^chamberlin-1935-2] What he learned there — that grain can be
taken, that expertise is presumptively disloyal, and that a personal following
inside an institution is worth more than an office — is what he did with the
country afterwards.

**The officers.** The Red Army was built in 1918 out of men who had commanded
the Tsar's, with a commissar beside each one because nobody trusted them.
That structural suspicion never left the institution.
[Whitewood](1918-russian-civil-war:epilogue-event-military-purge)'s account of
1937 turns on it: "a long history of the Red Army being perceived as vulnerable
to subversion is central to understanding this military purge. When faced with
perceived plots in the military Stalin tended to lean towards restraint, but by
1937 he felt he could no longer hesitate, and finally cracked down on what he
saw as a compromised army."[^whitewood-2015]

[Tukhachevsky](person:tukhachevsky-mikhail) — who commanded the Western Front
at twenty-seven, stormed Kronstadt at twenty-eight and broke Tambov at
twenty-eight — was shot in June 1937. He had joined the Bolsheviks in 1918 as a
guards lieutenant of the old army. He was one of the military specialists, and
had been from the first week.

Four years later: "Suddenly and without warning, early on the morning of 22 June
1941, over three million Axis forces lunged across the Soviet state border and
commenced Hitler's infamous Operation Barbarossa."[^glantz-2001] They crossed a
frontier drawn at Riga in 1921 and moved west in 1939, and they were met by an
army whose senior commanders had mostly been promoted into dead men's places
since 1937.

**How much of 1941 is owed to 1937 is not a question this pack answers.** The
link that carries it is filed at low confidence and says why: the strongest
version of the claim was made after 1956 by officers with an interest in making
it, and the army of June 1941 was simultaneously doubling in size, re-equipping
and redeploying into newly annexed territory, any of which would have
disorganised it. What the pack asserts is the chain, not the proportion — and
the chain runs from a beaten detachment on an island in the Volga in August 1918
to the Bug in June 1941 without a break in it.
