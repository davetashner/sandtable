A wheel needs a pivot. The German plan turned its right wing through Belgium about a fixed left, and the fixed left rested on the ground in front of Nancy — which meant that everything the right wing did depended on nobody pushing the pivot in.

Castelnau's 2nd Army comes back onto the Grand Couronné, the ridge of wooded hills east and north of Nancy, in the last week of August, beaten at Morhange and no longer capable of attacking anything. There it digs. The position is good — heights, forest, the Moselle behind the left, the fortress of Toul behind the right — and Castelnau's orders from Joffre are to cover Nancy, which is not quite the same as holding it.[^afgg-1-1]

What makes the next three weeks remarkable is what he does while holding. XVIII Corps has already been taken from him and railed to the Sambre; on 24 August IX Corps entrains for the 4th Army; XX Corps loses Foch, who goes west to form the army that will fight at Saint-Gond, and passes to Balfourier. Castelnau is holding the hinge of the whole French line while his army is being taken apart to feed the manoeuvre in the west — the same trains, the same timetable, running the other way from the German plan's.[^doughty-2005] [^herwig-2009]
