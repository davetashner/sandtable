The right wing's road runs through Liège, where twelve forts command the Meuse crossings. A coup de main on the night of 5–6 August fails bloodily against the forts, though [Ludendorff](person:ludendorff-erich), riding with the assault brigades, bluffs his way into the citadel on 7 August.[^herwig-2009]

The forts themselves hold until the heavy siege artillery arrives — Krupp's 42 cm howitzers and Austrian 30.5 cm mortars — and then fall one after another between 12 and 16 August.[^strachan-2001] Whether Liège "cost the timetable" is argued over: the 1st and 2nd Armies were not due to begin their march before 18 August in any case. But Belgian resistance, demolished railways and the need to mask Antwerp and Namur all begin to eat at a plan that had almost no slack.[^herwig-2009]
