It is remembered as a race to the sea. It was a race for a flank, and the sea only ended it.

Three times in three weeks Joffre takes an army out of the line, moves it north by rail, and sends it round what he believes is the German end: Castelnau into Picardy about Roye, then de Maud'huy's improvised Tenth Army at Arras. Each time the Germans are moving north by the same railways at the same speed, and each attempt to turn a flank simply lengthens the front.[^edmonds-1925] [^strachan-2001] On 4 October Joffre sends Foch north as his deputy to coordinate the French armies there, and to persuade — he has no authority over — the British and the Belgians.

Behind the northern flank, Antwerp is dying. Beseler's siege guns work through the forts as they had at Liège and Namur; a British naval brigade arrives on the 4th, too light to change the arithmetic. The Belgian field army leaves westward on 6–8 October rather than be shut inside, and the city surrenders on the 10th.[^edmonds-1925] [^keegan-1998]
