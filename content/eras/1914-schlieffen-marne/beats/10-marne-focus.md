**5 September.** Maunoury's 6th Army, moving east out of the Paris camp toward the Ourcq, runs into Kluck's flank guard, IV Reserve Corps, north-east of Meaux. Kluck, already across the Marne and chasing the BEF southward, begins pulling corps back north-west to face the new threat.[^herwig-2009]

**6–8 September.** The Ourcq fight hardens; reinforcements for Maunoury come out of Paris by rail and, famously, in requisitioned taxis. Every corps Kluck swings back widens the gap between his army and Bülow's — thirty kilometres by the 8th — into which the BEF and Franchet d'Espèrey's 5th Army advance, slowly, over the Petit and Grand Morin. At the marshes of Saint-Gond, Foch's 9th Army holds against Bülow's left and Hausen's night attack.[^edmonds-1922]

**9 September.** Moltke's emissary, Lieutenant-Colonel Hentsch, reaches Bülow's headquarters and then Kluck's. With the BEF across the Marne behind him, Bülow orders his army back; Kluck, winning on the Ourcq, must conform. The right wing breaks off the battle and marches for the Aisne.[^herwig-2009]
