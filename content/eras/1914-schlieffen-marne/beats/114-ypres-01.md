The crisis passes and the attrition does not. For ten days the salient is attacked in one sector at a time and held by whoever is nearest.

On 5 November the 7th Division comes out of the line after sixteen days on the Menin road with 44 officers and 2,336 other ranks. It landed at Zeebrugge four weeks earlier with 400 officers and 12,000 men.[^edmonds-1925] Nothing was done wrong to produce that number. It is the price of holding a line with no reserves behind it against six divisions for your two.

What replaces the men is French. From the last week of October [d'Urbal](person:urbal-victor-d)'s divisions take over the northern face of the salient, and by early November more than half the troops in front of Ypres are French — the fact that the British telling of the battle most reliably leaves out.[^edmonds-1925] [^strachan-2001]

North of Dixmude nothing needs holding any more. The German corps west of the Yser have pulled back across it, because a metre of salt water is arriving twice a day and no army can attack through a tide.[^van-pul-2006] [^belgian-army-report-1915] On 10 November Dixmude itself is given up, and [Ronarc'h](person:ronarch-pierre)'s brigade goes to the west bank with a fraction of the six thousand it brought.
