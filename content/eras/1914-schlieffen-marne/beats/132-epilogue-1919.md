The war ended in November 1918 and the two things that made 1919 a year in the history of physics were both already under way.

In a room at Manchester, Ernest Rutherford had spent the war firing alpha particles into gases when he was not on the Admiralty's Board of Invention and Research working on the detection of submarines.[^wilson-1983] In June 1919 he published four papers reporting that when the gas was nitrogen, something came out the far side with the range and the deflection of a hydrogen nucleus.[^rutherford-1919] He had broken a piece off a nucleus: the first artificial transmutation of one element into another. What the reaction actually was — the alpha absorbed, an isotope of oxygen left behind, rather than a chip knocked loose — was settled by Blackett's cloud-chamber photographs six years later.[^blackett-1925] His biographer records him excusing a missed meeting of the submarine board on the grounds that he had been disintegrating the nucleus, which was of greater consequence than the war; the staging of the remark varies in the telling and it is undated.[^eve-1939]

Six weeks earlier, on 29 May 1919, two British expeditions had photographed the stars around the eclipsed Sun — one at Sobral in Brazil, one on the island of Príncipe in the Gulf of Guinea — to see whether the Sun's gravity bent starlight, and by how much. Einstein's theory required about 1.75 seconds of arc. A Newtonian light corpuscle gives half of that.

Three numbers were published. The Sobral 4-inch telescope gave 1.98″ ± 0.12″; Príncipe gave 1.61″ ± 0.30″; the Sobral astrographic plates gave 0.93″ and were set aside as unreliable, the mirror having apparently changed figure in the heat.[^dyson-eddington-davidson-1920] The popular version of 1919 is a clean confirmation, and it is not one. Earman and Glymour argued in 1980 that a theory had been allowed to choose its own evidence.[^earman-glymour-1980] Kennefick answers that the decision to discard was Dyson's, made on instrumental grounds by the man least invested in the outcome, and Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody's re-reduction of the plates supports the published result.[^kennefick-2019] [^gilmore-tausch-pebody-2022] A reader who wants a verdict will have to read all three and pick.

What is not in dispute is what happened next. The results were announced on 6 November 1919 at a joint meeting of the Royal Society and the Royal Astronomical Society; the next morning *The Times* ran 'Revolution in Science… Newtonian Ideas Overthrown', and within a week Einstein was the most famous scientist alive.[^crelinsten-2006] A British expedition had confirmed a German's theory five years after the two countries went to war over a road through Belgium, and six months after the peace was signed at Versailles.
