Thirty-seven days separate the two shots fired in a Bosnian street from the German columns crossing into Belgium. Nothing in that interval was inevitable, and almost everything in it was decided by a few dozen men who believed they were responding to necessity.

A Bosnian Serb student, [Gavrilo Princip](person:princip-gavrilo), shot the Archduke [Franz Ferdinand](person:franz-ferdinand-of-austria) and his wife Sophie at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. The heir to the Habsburg throne had been one of the more cautious voices in Vienna about war with Serbia; his death removed the last restraint on the men who wanted one.[^clark-2012] Within a week the decision in Vienna was taken. What remained was to secure Berlin, and to write demands Belgrade could not accept.

Berlin gave its answer on 5 and 6 July, before it had seen those demands: Germany would stand by Austria-Hungary, whatever Vienna chose to do. Historians have argued about that assurance — the 'blank cheque' — for a century. To Fritz Fischer it was a decision for a continental war in pursuit of world power.[^fischer-1967] To Christopher Clark it was a conventional alliance guarantee given by a government that badly misread a local quarrel.[^clark-2012] What is not disputed is that it was given first and qualified never.

The note delivered at Belgrade on 23 July gave Serbia forty-eight hours. Serbia accepted nearly all of it and, minutes before the deadline, reserved the one demand that would have put Austro-Hungarian officials inside a Serbian judicial inquiry — offering, if that was not enough, to put the whole dispute to the tribunal at The Hague. Vienna had already decided that only total acceptance would do. It declared war on 28 July and shelled Belgrade the next day.[^albertini-1952]

From there the crisis stopped being about Serbia. Russia would not stand aside a second time after the humiliation of 1908; [Nicholas II](person:nicholas-ii) signed a general mobilization on 29 July, cancelled it after a telegram from his cousin the Kaiser, and signed it again on the 30th. German war plans treated Russian mobilization as the starting gun, and Moltke pressed hard for the answering order.[^mombauer-2001] Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August and on France on the 3rd.

And here the geography turns. The quarrel was between Vienna and St Petersburg, in the Balkans; the first shots of the war in the West were fired at Liège, five hundred miles away, against a country with no part in any of it. That happened because Germany had one deployment plan and it faced west: the answer to a two-front war was to beat France quickly, and the road ran through Belgium.[^strachan-2001] On the evening of 2 August Brussels was handed a note demanding free passage. It refused within twelve hours. On the morning of 4 August the columns crossed anyway, and Britain — whose divided cabinet had spent the week arguing about France — found in the violated treaty of 1839 the one cause that would carry Parliament.[^hastings-2013]

Read forwards, each step looks like a response to the step before. Read as a whole, the chain has at least five places where a different choice was available and was actually proposed: Vienna could have published its evidence and stopped; Berlin could have attached conditions to its assurance; Serbia's answer could have been accepted, as the Kaiser himself said it should be; the mediation offers of 26–29 July could have been taken up; Russia could have held at partial mobilization. Every one of those was on the table, and every one was declined by men who thought the risk of doing nothing was greater.
