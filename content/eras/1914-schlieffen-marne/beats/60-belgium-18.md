Liège has fallen and the 1st Army is over the Gette. On the evening of 18 August the King, with no French or British force within reach, orders the field army to break off and go back into Antwerp — the national redoubt of two rings of forts — rather than lose it in front of Brussels. By the 20th four divisions and the cavalry are inside the outer forts; Brussels is an open city and Kluck's corps march through it and keep wheeling south-west.[^edmonds-1925] [^strachan-2001]

One does not. Beseler's III Reserve Corps turns north of Brussels to stand between the Belgian army and the roads and railways the wheel now depends on — the first corps the right wing loses to Belgium, and it never rejoins. Behind the front the Belgians have blown what they could: the tunnels and bridges that keep the railheads a hundred kilometres behind the marching columns for weeks — a greater cause of delay, Strachan argues, than the forts themselves.[^herwig-2009] [^strachan-2001]

At Aarschot on the 19th, after a fight with the rearguard, German troops shoot 156 inhabitants and burn the town: the first large reprisal, and the pattern — a conviction that civilians are firing, then collective punishment — that will repeat for ten days.[^horne-kramer-2001]
