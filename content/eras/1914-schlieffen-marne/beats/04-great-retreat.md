Joffre's Instruction générale no 2 of 25 August abandons the offensive: the armies will retreat toward the Seine and a new army will be built on the left, by rail, from the Lorraine front.[^afgg-1-1] The British fight a second stand at Le Cateau on 26 August; Lanrezac turns and strikes Bülow at Guise on 29 August, buying a day.[^edmonds-1922]

On the German side the right wing is already thinner than the plan assumed. Moltke has strengthened the left wing in Lorraine, corps have been left to mask Antwerp and Maubeuge, and on 25–26 August two more are sent to East Prussia.[^herwig-2009] Believing the French left beaten and anxious to keep touch with Bülow, Kluck wheels the 1st Army south-east on 30–31 August — passing east of Paris instead of west, and exposing his outer flank to the Paris garrison and to the new 6th Army forming there.[^reichsarchiv-weltkrieg-1]
