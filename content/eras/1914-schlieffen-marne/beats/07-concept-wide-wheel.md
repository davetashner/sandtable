**This branch is hypothetical.** It draws the sweep as the 1905 memorandum imagined it: from 25 August the 1st Army, at full strength, keeps swinging west and south through Amiens, to pass _outside_ Paris rather than turning short of it — the fortified capital as a pivot, not a target.[^herwig-2009]

What it does not draw is the rest of the war: the French armies stay where history put them, nothing is said about supply or march rates, and no outcome is claimed. The branch renders a geometry. Whether that geometry was ever a plan is argued — Zuber holds the memorandum was a staff study and 'the [Schlieffen](person:schlieffen-alfred-von) Plan' a post-war construction;[^zuber-2002] Mombauer and others reply that Moltke inherited and modified a real operational concept.[^mombauer-2001] The branch 'What success required' asks the next question: what would have had to be true for the geometry to become an event.
