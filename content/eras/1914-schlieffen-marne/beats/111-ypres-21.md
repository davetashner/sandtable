Two attacks go in at once, twenty kilometres apart, and both are made by formations that were civilians in July.

On the northern face of the salient the Fourth Army's new reserve corps come on between Bixschoote and Langemarck: ten weeks old, short of training and shorter of officers, advancing in close order against regular infantry firing fifteen aimed rounds a minute. They gain a few hundred metres over four days and lose very heavily.[^edmonds-1925]

This is the fighting that Germany would remember as the *Kindermord bei Ypern*. Almost nothing in the memory is true of these days: the men were mostly not students and mostly not boys, the sentence that made them famous was written three weeks later about different ground, and the singing has no source but the sentence.[^unruh-1986] [^1914-1918-online-langemarck] The card below carries the argument; the beat that carries the sentence is three weeks downstream, which is where it belongs.

Meanwhile, on the night of the 21st, the XXII Reserve Corps gets across the Yser at the Tervaete bend. Over five days the Belgians give up the far bank entirely and fall back onto a railway embankment a metre and a half high — the last defensible thing in front of the ports.[^van-pul-2006] [^belgian-army-report-1915]
