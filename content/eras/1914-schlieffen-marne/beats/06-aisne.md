The German 1st and 2nd Armies fall back some sixty to eighty kilometres to the heights north of the Aisne and dig in; the Allied pursuit, cautious and tired, reaches the river on 13 September and cannot cross in strength.[^herwig-2009] Moltke is replaced by Falkenhayn on 14 September.

Both sides then reach north for each other's open flank — the "race to the sea" — until the line runs from the Swiss border to the Channel. The mobile war that Schlieffen's timetable had been designed to win quickly ends instead in the stalemate it was meant to prevent.[^strachan-2001]
