There is fog on the morning of the 22nd, and the battle begins everywhere at once as an accident. It is not one battle but six or seven, fought at ranges of a few hundred metres by units that have no idea where the rest of their army is.[^zuber-2007]

At **Rossignol**, on the Semois, the 3rd Colonial Division walks into the German VI Corps. The _marsouins_ are the best infantry France has — long-service colonial regiments out of Tonkin and Morocco — and they attack, repeatedly, against machine guns and field artillery already in position on the far bank. General [Raffenel](person:raffenel-leon) is killed, his brigade commander Rondony with him, and the Colonial Corps loses 11,648 men in the day.[^greenhalgh-2014] At **Bertrix**, XVII Corps is caught in column in the woods and driven back without most of its divisional artillery — guns that were never unlimbered, because there was nowhere on a forest track to unlimber them.[^zuber-2007] At **Ethe** and **Bleid** the columns meet in village streets in fog too thick for either side's guns to fire. Only on the left, at **Maissin** and **Anloy**, do the French take and hold what they attacked for; they give it up the next morning with everything else.[^afgg-1-1]

The pattern is the same across the whole front, and it is not a matter of courage. German infantry lies down and shoots; German field artillery gets off the road and into action; French infantry, trained to close and dressed in blue coats and red trousers, attacks across open ground into it. This is the day on which about 27,000 French soldiers die along the whole line from the Vosges to the Sambre — the bloodiest day in the history of France, and the country will not learn the number for a generation.[^herwig-2009]
