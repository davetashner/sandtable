The pursuit is slow — the Allied armies are as tired as the Germans, the roads are choked and it rains — and the 1st and 2nd Armies break contact. By the evening of the 11th they are across the Aisne between Compiègne and Reims; by the 13th, when the British and French come up, the Germans are dug in on the heights north of the river and the attacks of the next days fail.[^herwig-2009] [^edmonds-1922]

The Schlieffen timetable is dead: the French army is intact, Paris is safe, and the war of movement in the West is over. What remains of 1914 is the Race to the Sea, Antwerp, the Yser and Ypres — and, from the North Sea to Switzerland, the trench line.[^herwig-2009]
