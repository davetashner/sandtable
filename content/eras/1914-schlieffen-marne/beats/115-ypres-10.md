On 11 November two things happen, and only one of them is a battle.

From half past six, twelve and a half German divisions attack on a nine-mile front from Messines to the Menin road. The Guard breaks the 1st (Guards) Brigade's line and pushes into Nonne Bosschen — Nuns' Wood — where it meets the 2nd Oxfordshire and Buckinghamshire Light Infantry and, behind them, cooks, orderlies, engineers and gunners' details, put into the gap because there was nobody else to put there. By the afternoon the Guard is out again.[^edmonds-1925] It is the last serious German attempt of the year in the West. That night [FitzClarence](person:fitzclarence-charles), who found the Worcesters eleven days before, is killed leading a counter-attack near Polygon Wood.[^edmonds-1925]

The other thing is a sentence. The Supreme Command's communiqué of the same day reports young regiments going forward west of Langemarck singing the national anthem and taking the enemy's first line. It describes November fighting, not the October battle it would afterwards be attached to; the men of those corps were mostly neither students nor boys; and the singing rests on the sentence itself.[^unruh-1986] [^1914-1918-online-langemarck] Out of it came a national legend, and Germany marched under it for thirty years.
