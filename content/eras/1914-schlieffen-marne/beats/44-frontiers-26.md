At two in the morning Smith-Dorrien decides that II Corps, with the 4th Division and the 19th Brigade beside it, cannot get away and must fight at daybreak — against GHQ's order to keep retiring. From dawn to mid-afternoon they hold [Kluck](person:kluck-alexander-von)'s IV and III Corps and Marwitz's cavalry in the open fields around Le Cateau, lose some 7,800 men and 38 guns, and then break off and march south. Kluck, sure he has beaten the whole BEF, turns south-west after an army that is not there.[^edmonds-1922] [^herwig-2009] [^hastings-2013]

That afternoon at Saint-Quentin [Joffre](person:joffre-joseph) sits between [Sir John French](person:french-john) and [Lanrezac](person:lanrezac-charles), who will not speak to each other, and asks both to keep retreating in line while he builds a new left wing by rail; Lanrezac is to turn and strike at Guise. Whether Le Cateau saved the BEF or wasted a division is argued — Smith-Dorrien was removed the next spring, and the Official History is on his side.[^edmonds-1922] [^tuchman-1962]
