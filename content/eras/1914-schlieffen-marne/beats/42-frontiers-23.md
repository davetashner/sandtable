Kluck's III, IV and IX Corps come at the canal at Mons in close order and are shot flat by riflemen firing fifteen aimed rounds a minute — fire the Germans take for massed machine guns; by the afternoon the salient at Nimy and weight of numbers force II Corps back a few kilometres, in order. The same day Hausen's Saxons force the Meuse at Dinant against Franchet d'Espèrey's counter-attack at Onhaye, and shoot 674 of the town's people.[^edmonds-1922] [^herwig-2009] [^horne-kramer-2001]

With Bülow across the Sambre in front and Hausen across the Meuse behind his right, Lanrezac orders the 5th Army back at nine that night — and does not tell the British. Sir John French hears of it from a liaison officer the next morning; his army must retreat too or be enveloped, and he never forgives the Frenchman. Whether Lanrezac saved his army or abandoned his ally is still argued; both are true.[^herwig-2009] [^tuchman-1962]
