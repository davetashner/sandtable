For the fortnight that decides the campaign, Belgium holds what the plan could not spare. The IX Reserve Corps, released from Schleswig once a British landing on the North Sea coast was ruled out, detrains at Brussels on the 28th and takes over part of the line facing Antwerp; only when the Marine Division and Landwehr brigades come up at the beginning of September is it free to march, and it reaches the Ourcq on 9 September — the last day. The III Reserve Corps never leaves the Antwerp front.[^herwig-2009] [^edmonds-1925] [^reichsarchiv-weltkrieg-1]

At Maubeuge the siege guns open on the 29th. The forts are old and under-gunned and the garrison is mostly reservists and Territorials, and it holds for nine days of bombardment — longer than Liège, three times longer than Namur. Fournier surrenders on the 7th: some forty thousand men and four hundred guns by the British count, 32,700 prisoners by Herwig's, 45,000 by Lodier's. He is court-martialled for it, like the other governors who capitulated, and acquitted in 1920.[^herwig-2009] [^edmonds-1922] [^tyng-1935]

The arithmetic is the lesson: three corps north of the Sambre on the day Kluck turns inside Paris, two of them still there on the day Maunoury finds his flank. The plan had needed them on the outer wing; the right wing marched on without them.[^herwig-2009]
