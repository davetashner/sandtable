They were assigned to Hindenburg on 31 August, the day after the battle they had been sent to fight ended. They went into the line for the Masurian Lakes on 7–14 September, which pushed Rennenkampf out of East Prussia in the same week that, thirteen hundred kilometres away, the gap between the German First and Second Armies was being found on the Marne.[^showalter-2004] [^stone-1975]

That is the arithmetic this chapter exists to show. Two corps that stood on the wheel on 25 August were in the wrong hemisphere of the war by 5 September, and they decided neither of the two battles they might have decided.

Whether they would have decided the Marne is the most quoted might-have-been of 1914, and it is not settled here. Moltke's own circle treated the transfer as the fatal error. Against that: they were only part of a wider bleed — Antwerp, Maubeuge, Givet — and they came from the 2nd and 3rd Armies, not from the flank where the gap actually opened. Herwig puts the Marne down to the coordination of the German right and Joffre's redeployment rather than to any two corps.[^herwig-2009] [^mombauer-2001]
