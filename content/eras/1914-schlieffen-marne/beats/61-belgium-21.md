Namur was to be the anchor of Lanrezac's line and was supposed to hold for weeks. Gallwitz brings the Guard Reserve and XI Corps and the howitzers that broke Liège; the bombardment opens on the 21st, Cognelée and Marchovelette fall on the 23rd, and that night Michel's 4th Division slips out behind the French 5th Army — by rail through France to Le Havre, by sea to Ostend, and so round to Antwerp. The last forts surrender on the 25th. Four days.[^herwig-2009] [^strachan-2001] [^tyng-1935]

Along the Sambre and the Meuse the same days bring the worst of the reprisals. At Tamines on the 22nd, after the 2nd Army's crossing, 383 townspeople are shot on the riverbank; at Dinant on the 23rd [Hausen](person:hausen-max-von)'s Saxons, forcing the river under fire and certain the town is in arms, kill 674 civilians and burn the centre — the largest single killing of the invasion. Horne and Kramer, who went through the German as well as the Belgian records, found no organised civilian resistance anywhere: an army that had been taught to expect francs-tireurs saw them in every stray shot, and punished the town. The numbers are theirs.[^horne-kramer-2001]
