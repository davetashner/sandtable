Since the 29th, six divisions under [Fabeck](person:fabeck-max-von) have been attacking the eight kilometres from Gheluvelt to Messines behind the heaviest artillery yet put on this front.[^edmonds-1925] [^1914-1918-online-ypres]

At about eleven on the morning of the 31st the line on the Menin road goes. Gheluvelt is taken, two brigades are broken through, and for some hours there is nothing formed between the attack and Ypres four kilometres behind. Shortly after one o'clock a shell comes through the window of Hooge château, where the headquarters of the 1st and 2nd Divisions are sharing a room: Lomax is mortally wounded, Monro stunned, most of both staffs killed or hurt. The sector loses its command in the same hour it loses its front.[^edmonds-1925]

What saves it is a brigadier who happens to be standing there. [FitzClarence](person:fitzclarence-charles) goes looking for a reserve and finds one battalion — seven officers and 357 men of the 2nd Worcestershire. They cross a thousand yards of open ground in shellfire, lose over a hundred doing it, and go into the château grounds with the bayonet.[^edmonds-1925]

Ten kilometres south the Messines ridge goes the same night, held to the last by dismounted cavalry and the London Scottish. It stays German until June 1917.[^edmonds-1925]
