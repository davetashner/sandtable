At three in the morning [Hausen](person:hausen-max-von)'s Saxons and the Guard go forward with the bayonet and no artillery preparation against [Foch](person:foch-ferdinand)'s right at the eastern end of the marshes. The IX and XI Corps are thrown back several kilometres and Fère-Champenoise is lost; Foch feeds in his last reserves and begins to move the 42nd Division from his left to his right. It is the last German success of the battle, and it cannot be exploited.[^herwig-2009]

In the west the BEF is on the Petit Morin and the 5th Army takes Marchais-en-Brie; [Bülow](person:bulow-karl-von)'s right bends back. Moltke, in Luxembourg and nearly out of touch, sends Lieutenant-Colonel [Hentsch](person:hentsch-richard) by car to the five armies of the right and centre with authority — its extent disputed to this day — to coordinate a retreat if the gap cannot be closed. Hentsch reaches Bülow at Montmort at a quarter to eight in the evening and finds him already of the view that, should the British cross the Marne, the 2nd Army must go back.[^herwig-2009] [^tuchman-1962]
