The heavy artillery and the Guard begin to move east. Snow falls on the 19th. The attacks stop.

On 18 November [Falkenhayn](person:falkenhayn-erich) tells the Chancellor what the autumn has taught him: Germany can no longer defeat the whole coalition outright, and the object from now on is to detach one of its members by a separate peace.[^foley-2005] It is the first time the man running the war says so, and he says it three and a half months after the timetable was to have delivered a decision in the West.

What it cost to find out is on the casualty cards. 58,155 British casualties at Ypres; 89,864 since the landing in August, out of an expeditionary force of some 160,000, and most of it out of the infantry of seven divisions.[^edmonds-1925] German losses on this front over the same weeks are given by the Reichsarchiv as 134,315, under a caveat of its own that the missing were counted too high.[^edmonds-1925] [^1914-1918-online-ypres]

The salient stays within a few kilometres of where 22 November left it for two and a half years. Everything after this is the trench war, and the trench war starts here because there is nowhere left to go around.
