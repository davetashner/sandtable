Look at the map: the direct road from Germany into France was closed, and the French had spent forty years closing it.

After losing Alsace and much of Lorraine in 1871, France built the Séré de Rivières system along the new frontier — the fortified ridge from **Verdun** to **Toul**, and a second from **Épinal** to **Belfort**, with the gap between Toul and Épinal at Charmes left deliberately open as a trap into which an attacker could be channelled and then struck in the flank.[^doughty-2005] Behind them the Meuse and Moselle heights; in front, on the German side, Metz and Strasbourg, fortified in their turn.

Schlieffen's December 1905 memorandum states the conclusion in one line: a frontal attack on the Belfort–Verdun position offers little prospect of success.[^ritter-1958] Any German plan for a quick decision therefore had to go round — and there was only one way round. North of the Ardennes the country is open, the rivers run the wrong way for a defender, and the frontier belonged to Belgium.[^strachan-2001] [^herwig-2009]

The fortress line is the reason the war in the West began in a country that had no quarrel with anyone.
