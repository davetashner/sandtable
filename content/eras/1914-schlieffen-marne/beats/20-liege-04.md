Liège sits where the Meuse turns north towards Holland, and the railway and roads of the right wing's march run through it. Around the city, seven to nine kilometres out, stand twelve concrete forts built by Brialmont in the 1880s — the strongest fortress of its kind in Europe when they were built, and by 1914 a generation behind the guns being built to break them.[^herwig-2009] [^strachan-2001]

On the morning of 4 August, Belgium having refused passage, Emmich's six brigades and Marwitz's cavalry cross the frontier and close on the ring; [Leman](person:leman-gerard), with the 3rd Division and the fortress troops, refuses the German summons on the 5th, and the field guns open on the eastern forts. The plan wants the city and its bridges within days — the timetable's first test.[^herwig-2009] [^tuchman-1962]
