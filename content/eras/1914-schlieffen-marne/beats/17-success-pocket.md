**Hypothetical.** With the right wing passing west of Paris and the left wing holding the Metz hinge, the retreating French armies find the German 1st Army behind their left. The BEF, as [Sir John French](person:french-john) proposed on 31 August, retires south-west behind the Seine and out of the battle; the 5th Army falls back on Reims and the Marne with [Kluck](person:kluck-alexander-von) closing from the west and Bülow from the south-west; the 4th is pinned on the Verdun–Toul line. The concept's logic — [Schlieffen](person:schlieffen-alfred-von)'s borrowed Cannae — is a double envelopment: pin the centre, encircle with the wings.[^herwig-2009]

The same sources that describe the idea describe why it strains belief: the outer wing has to march farther and faster than the historical 1st Army already did — some thirty kilometres a day for a fortnight — on roads and railways the Belgians had wrecked, with corps that had in fact been sent elsewhere, against an enemy who in reality kept retreating rather than standing to be enclosed.[^herwig-2009] [^strachan-2001] Every one of those is unmet on the checklist.
