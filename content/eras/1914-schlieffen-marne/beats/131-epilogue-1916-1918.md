The November papers travelled. One copy reached an artillery unit on the Russian front, where a forty-two-year-old volunteer was computing trajectories.

Karl Schwarzschild had been director of the observatory at Potsdam and had volunteered in August 1914. Within weeks of reading Einstein he had done what Einstein had not expected anyone to do quickly: found an exact solution of the field equations, for the field outside a single spherical mass. He sent it to Berlin in a letter of 22 December 1915, and Einstein read it to the Academy on his behalf on 13 January 1916.[^cpae-8] [^schwarzschild-1916] By then Schwarzschild had contracted pemphigus, an autoimmune disease of the skin, at the front. He was invalided home in March and died on 11 May 1916.[^schwarzschild-werke-1992] The radius that carries his name was, for half a century, treated as an artefact of the coordinates.

The other loose end from November 1915 was mathematical, and it took three years. General relativity had broken something that every previous theory of physics had kept: the conservation of energy no longer behaved as it had. Hilbert and Klein had brought Emmy Noether to Göttingen in 1915 partly to sort it out.[^kosmann-schwarzbach-2011]

Her answer, presented on 26 July 1918, is more general than the problem. Every continuous symmetry of a physical law carries a conserved quantity with it — time-invariance gives you energy, translation gives you momentum, rotation gives you angular momentum — and a theory with the symmetry general relativity has gets a conservation law of a different kind, which is why the old one appeared to fail.[^noether-1918] [^kosmann-schwarzbach-2011] Klein read it to the Göttingen scientific society, because Noether was not a member and could not read it herself. She had been lecturing under Hilbert's name since 1915; the university put her on the payroll in 1923.[^dick-1981]
