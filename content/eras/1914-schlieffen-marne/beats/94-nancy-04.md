The German 6th Army attacks the whole position at one o'clock on 4 September — the same afternoon on which the wireless intercepts and the aircraft reports of Kluck's turn south-east are being read at Paris and at Joffre's headquarters. Two battles are about to be fought at the same hour, two hundred kilometres apart, and only one of them is the one the plan wanted.[^herwig-2009]

The weight falls on the forest of Champenoux and the Amance plateau. On 6 September more than three thousand heavy shells fall on Amance in eight hours; the infantry that follows does not take it. This is the largest concentration of German artillery anywhere in Lorraine, and it is firing at a hill.[^afgg-1-1]

The point is not that the attack fails — attacks on entrenched heights usually did. The point is where the guns are. Since the middle of August the German 6th and 7th Armies have been the strongest force in the German army west of the Rhine, and in the week the campaign is decided they are in Lorraine, attacking fortifications the plan had meant to walk around, against an army that keeps sending corps away from the fight to somewhere else.[^doughty-2005]
