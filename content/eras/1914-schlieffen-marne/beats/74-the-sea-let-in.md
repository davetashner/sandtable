The Belgians are being pushed off the Yser, so they use the only weapon left in the country: the sea they have spent eight centuries keeping out.

At Nieuwpoort eight channels meet at a lock complex called the Ganzepoot. Men who work the polders for a living know which gates will flood the plain — [Karel Cogge](person:cogge-karel), water bailiff of the Veurne polders, and [Hendrik Geeraert](person:geeraert-hendrik), a bargeman who can find a sluice in the dark. On successive nights of the last week of October they open the gates to the flood tide and shut them to the ebb. By 30 October the water is four to five kilometres wide, the German positions are islands, and the attack across the Yser is over for good.[^van-pul-2006]

Ten kilometres south the new German reserve corps attack towards Langemarck and lose heavily for very little ground. The communiqué that made those losses famous came three weeks later, described different fighting, and turned half-trained reservists into singing schoolboys.[^unruh-1986] [^1914-1918-online-langemarck]
