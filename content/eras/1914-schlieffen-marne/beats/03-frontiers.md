French Plan XVII throws the 1st and 2nd Armies into Lorraine on 14 August, straight at the German left — very nearly the trap Schlieffen had imagined. Rupprecht's 6th Army absorbs the blow and counterattacks at Morhange–Sarrebourg on 20 August; the French fall back on Nancy and the Meurthe.[^afgg-1-1]

On the Allied left the German right wing arrives. Lanrezac, who alone among the French army commanders has read the wheel for what it is, has the 5th Army moved north to the angle of the Sambre and the Meuse; on 17 August he meets Sir John French at Rethel for the first time, and the two commanders-in-chief of the Allied left take against each other within the hour.[^spears-1930] On 21 August Bülow's 2nd Army reaches the Sambre at Charleroi and Hausen's 3rd the Meuse at Dinant; the same day Joffre sends the 3rd and 4th Armies forward into the Ardennes, where he believes the German centre to be weak.[^herwig-2009] The British, two corps strong, are coming up from Maubeuge towards Mons.[^edmonds-1922]
