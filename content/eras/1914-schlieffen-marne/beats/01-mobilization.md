Germany declares war on Russia on 1 August and on France on 3 August, and on the morning of 4 August its troops cross into Belgium, which has refused passage.[^herwig-2009] Seven armies — roughly a million and a half men — deploy by rail against France; five of them, the right wing and centre, will wheel west and south through Belgium and Luxembourg, deliberately passing north of the French frontier fortresses at Verdun, Toul, Épinal and Belfort.[^strachan-2001]

The plan's whole logic is time. Russia is expected to need six weeks to mobilize; France must be beaten before then, so that the armies can be entrained east.[^tuchman-1962] Everything that follows is measured against that timetable.
