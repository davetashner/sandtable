The pursuit from the Marne stops at a river and a ridge. On the heights of the Chemin des Dames the German right wing turns and stands, stiffened by the reserve corps that force-marched up from the ruins of Maubeuge; the French and British get across the Aisne but not through it.[^edmonds-1922]

Then, in a fortnight, the armies do by exhaustion what no plan had provided for. Attacks that would have worked in August fail against men who are below ground, so both sides go below ground: continuous trench, wire, guns registered on the ground in front. Fifty years of firepower arrives at its conclusion — infantry in the open cannot cross ground swept by magazine rifles, machine guns and quick-firing artillery, and infantry underground is very hard to remove.[^strachan-2001]

The consequence is strategic rather than tactical. A front without gaps cannot be turned where it stands; it can only be extended. From the third week of September the only open flank left in France lies to the north, and both sides begin walking towards it.[^herwig-2009]
