On 9 September sixty-seven heavy shells fall on the centre of Nancy and kill eight civilians. The city takes it as the beginning of a siege. It is the opposite: the guns are firing to cover a withdrawal that has already started.[^afgg-1-1]

On the 11th and 12th the German 6th Army goes back behind the Seille, and French patrols push east into a forest that is empty. The front stops there. Apart from a few kilometres it will not move again until 1918 — the first stretch of the continuous line that is about to run from Switzerland to the sea.[^tyng-1935]

The Grand Couronné is not a famous battle and it did not decide anything by itself. What it did was refuse to move. Every counterfactual in which the German plan works needs the French left to give way or to stay stuck out in Lorraine where it began; instead the French pivot held its ground while lending corps to the decision elsewhere, and the German pivot spent September attacking it. A plan is a bet about time, and part of what the German bet needed was that the enemy would keep obliging. Here, at the hinge, he stopped.[^herwig-2009] [^doughty-2005]
