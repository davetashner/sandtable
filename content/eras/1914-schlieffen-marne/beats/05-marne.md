French and British reconnaissance — cavalry, and the new aeroplanes — see Kluck's columns crossing the Marne and moving south-east across the front of Paris.[^herwig-2009] Gallieni, the city's governor, presses Joffre to strike; on 4 September Joffre orders the counter-offensive for the 6th. Maunoury's 6th Army attacks Kluck's flank along the Ourcq on 5 September; Kluck swings corps after corps back north-west to meet it, and a gap of thirty kilometres opens between his army and Bülow's.[^afgg-1-1]

Into that gap, slowly, move the BEF and Franchet d'Espèrey's 5th Army; at the marshes of Saint-Gond, Foch's 9th Army holds Hausen and Bülow's left.[^edmonds-1922] On 8–9 September Moltke's emissary, Hentsch, visits the right-wing headquarters; Bülow, his flank open, orders a retreat, and Kluck must follow. Six weeks into the campaign, the exhausted German right wing turns back.[^herwig-2009]
