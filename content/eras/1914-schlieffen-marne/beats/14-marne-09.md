The British cross the Marne at Charly and Nanteuil-sur-Marne in the morning — at La Ferté-sous-Jouarre the bridges are down and the III Corps needs another day — and German airmen report their columns north of the river. Bülow orders the 2nd Army back towards Dormans and Épernay.[^edmonds-1922] [^herwig-2009]

Hentsch drives west through the 2nd Army's retreating right and reaches Kluck's headquarters at Mareuil-sur-Ourcq late in the morning. Kluck, whose IX Corps is at that moment about to turn Maunoury's left at Nanteuil, is told that the 2nd Army is retreating and that the 1st must fall back to the Aisne. He obeys. Whether Hentsch held the authority to order a retreat, whether Bülow had already decided, and whether the 1st Army could have won on the Ourcq before the gap undid it, have been argued since 1914; the army's own inquiry in 1917 found Hentsch had acted within his instructions.[^herwig-2009] [^tuchman-1962]
