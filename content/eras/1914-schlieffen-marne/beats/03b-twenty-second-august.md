In the fog of the Ardennes the French 3rd and 4th Armies, advancing in column on a front of eighty kilometres, walk into the German 4th and 5th, which are advancing too. There are no flanks and no plan on either side: at Rossignol, Neufchâteau, Bertrix, Virton and Ethe, regiments meet regiments in woods and rye and the side that deploys its guns and lies down first wins. The French, trained to attack and dressed to be seen, mostly do neither; by nightfall the 3rd Colonial Division has been destroyed at Rossignol, the 4th Army's centre is broken at Bertrix, and both armies are falling back to the Meuse.[^herwig-2009] [^afgg-1-1]

On the same day Bülow's 2nd Army is across the Sambre on either side of Charleroi and Hausen's Saxons are fighting for the Meuse bridges at Dinant; in Lorraine the 1st and 2nd Armies are still retreating from Morhange. Along the whole line from the Vosges to the Sambre about 27,000 French soldiers die in the day — the bloodiest single day in the history of the French army, and of France. The figure is a modern reconstruction from the army's own death records; the men who compiled the monthly returns in the retreat did not know it, and the country did not learn it for a generation.[^steg-2013] [^hastings-2013]
