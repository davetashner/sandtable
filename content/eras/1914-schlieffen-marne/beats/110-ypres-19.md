Between the North Sea and the Lys there are forty kilometres of Flanders that nobody holds, and this is the last of them anywhere in Europe.

North to south on 19 October: King Albert's field army on the Yser embankment from Nieuwpoort to Dixmude, six divisions that have been retreating for ten weeks and have lost their depots with their country; [Ronarc'h](person:ronarch-pierre)'s naval brigade in Dixmude itself; [Rawlinson](person:rawlinson-henry)'s IV Corps in Ypres, which reached Belgium too late to save Antwerp; [Haig](person:haig-douglas)'s I Corps detraining behind it; Allenby's cavalry holding the Messines ridge on foot.[^edmonds-1925]

Coming the other way is a Fourth Army rebuilt out of four reserve corps raised in August, and the Sixth Army up from Lille.[^strachan-2001]

On the 21st Haig is ordered to advance north-east on Bruges. GHQ believes there is one German corps in front of him. There are four, and two more behind them. His corps makes something over a kilometre and digs in where it stopped.[^edmonds-1925] It is the last order to advance given on this ground in 1914, and the British do not attack here again until 1917.
