On 23 August [Lanrezac](person:lanrezac-charles)'s 5th Army, with Bülow across the Sambre in front of it and Hausen across the Meuse on its right, is in danger of being enveloped from both sides; that evening Lanrezac orders a retreat without telling the British on his left.[^herwig-2009] The same day the BEF's II Corps has fought Kluck's 1st Army along the Mons–Condé canal — the British army's first battle in western Europe since Waterloo — and held it for a day at a cost of some 1,600 men; learning overnight that the French have gone, [Sir John French](person:french-john) orders his own retreat on the 24th.[^edmonds-1922] Namur's forts, which were to have held for months, begin to fall to the same heavy howitzers that took Liège.[^herwig-2009]

By 24 August every Allied army from Belgium to the Vosges is falling back. [Joffre](person:joffre-joseph), who on the 21st still believed the German centre weak, writes to the Minister of War that night that the army is 'condemned to a defensive attitude' and must hold on its fortresses and the obstacles of the ground while it is reorganised; the instruction that turns the retreat into a plan follows on the 25th.[^afgg-1-1]
