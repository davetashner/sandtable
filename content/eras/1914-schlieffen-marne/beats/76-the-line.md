By the end of November the trenches run from Nieuwpoort on the North Sea to the Swiss frontier at Pfetterhouse: some seven hundred kilometres, and not one open flank at either end.[^strachan-2001] [^keegan-1998]

The plan had asked for a decision in the West in about six weeks, so that the army could turn east. Sixteen weeks after the first troops crossed into Belgium there is no decision, no turning east, and no way round. Everything the timetable had been built to avoid — a long war, a war on two fronts, a war of matériel against an enemy with more of it — is now the war that will be fought.

Nothing about the line was decided in November. It was decided by every day the plan ran late: eleven days at Liège, corps left at Antwerp and Maubeuge, two corps entrained for East Prussia, railheads that never caught up with the boots. The map at the end is the sum of that arithmetic, and it will not move more than fifteen kilometres in either direction for three and a half years.[^edmonds-1925]

Four more years of it are coming, and so is something else, which has a chapter of its own rather than a paragraph here. The campaign clock stops on this date; the ✦ Meanwhile layer does not. **Meanwhile, 1915–1919** is below, and it is the only level in this pack that keeps a window of its own — five years instead of forty days, because that is how long the rest of it takes. It is not about the fighting, and that is the point of it.
