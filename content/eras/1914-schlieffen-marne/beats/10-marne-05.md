Kluck's army is across the Marne and marching south-east after the British, its right flank towards Paris covered by a single corps, Gronau's IV Reserve, strung out north-east of Meaux. Gallieni's aviators have been reporting the German columns crossing the front of the capital for two days.[^herwig-2009]

At midday Maunoury's 6th Army, moving east out of the Paris entrenched camp a day earlier than Joffre intended, runs into that flank guard around Monthyon and Saint-Soupplets. Gronau attacks the leading French reserve divisions, holds them through the afternoon, and in the night falls back behind the Thérouanne — and reports.[^herwig-2009] [^afgg-1-1] By ten that evening Kluck has ordered the II Corps back north across the Marne. The battle he is about to fight is not the one he was marching towards.[^herwig-2009]
