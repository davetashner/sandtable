The whole plan rested on the East holding with almost nothing, and on 20 August the East stopped holding. Seven German armies had gone west; one, the Eighth, was left to cover a province against two Russian armies that had come in from the east and the south, on either side of a belt of lakes. Attacking Rennenkampf in the open at Gumbinnen, the Eighth Army was fought to a standstill.[^showalter-2004]

Tactically the day settled nothing. What it settled was a temperament. That evening Prittwitz telephoned Moltke to say he meant to break off and retire behind the Vistula — to give up East Prussia, Königsberg and the ground the eastern deployment existed to hold. His staff talked him most of the way back within hours, but the alarm had gone in.[^showalter-2004] [^mombauer-2001]

Moltke relieved him on the 22nd, called Hindenburg out of retirement and gave him Ludendorff, still lit by Liège, as chief of staff. They reached Marienburg on the afternoon of the 23rd and adopted the concentration Hoffmann had already begun.[^showalter-2004] [^stone-1975]
