The Provisional Government's answer to the question of the war was to fight it. [Kerensky](person:kerensky-alexander), as Minister of War, spent May and June touring the front making speeches, and on 18 June (O.S.) the South-Western Front attacked in Galicia. It went well for about a week — the Austrians gave ground, there were prisoners and captured guns — and then the German counter-attack came in and the front dissolved. Units held meetings and voted not to move; whole divisions walked away.[^stone-1975]

Something had been settled by that. The February settlement rested on a bargain that could not be stated out loud: the soldiers had made the revolution, and the revolution would end the war. The offensive said, as clearly as anything could, that it would not.

Ten days later Petrograd came out. On 3 July (O.S.) the machine-gun regiment on the Vyborg side decided to march on the Tauride Palace and demand that the Soviet take the power it kept refusing; the Kronstadt sailors came over the water the next morning; by the afternoon of the 4th something like half a million people were in the streets, most of them armed.[^rabinowitch-1976]

What they wanted was clear enough — *all power to the Soviets* — and the men they were demanding it of, the Soviet's own Menshevik and SR leadership, did not want it. What is not clear, and has never been settled, is what the Bolsheviks were doing. The party's Military Organisation had been pushing for exactly this; its Central Committee had not; Lenin arrived back from a few days' rest in Finland in the middle of it, spoke briefly and unenthusiastically from a balcony, and spent the rest of the crisis trying to bring the crowd down without disowning it.[^rabinowitch-1976] Rabinowitch's account — that the party spent three days running to catch up with its own supporters — is the one that has held up best.

It ended in rain, in shooting on the Nevsky by persons unknown, and in a document. On the evening of 4 July the government released material accusing Lenin of being a German agent, and the loyal troops it had summoned from the front arrived the next morning. The demonstration went home. *Pravda* was wrecked, Trotsky and others were gaoled, and Lenin shaved his beard and went over the Finnish border, where he stayed for three months.

For about six weeks the Bolsheviks were finished. It is worth stopping on that, because the story is usually told as an ascent. In the middle of July 1917 the party's leader was a fugitive accused of treason, its press was closed, its second figure was in prison, and its support in the Petrograd Soviet was falling.

Whether the accusation was true is [a question with a documentary answer and a contested one](1917-russian-revolution:historiography-german-gold).
