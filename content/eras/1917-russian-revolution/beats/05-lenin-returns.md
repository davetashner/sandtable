[Lenin](person:lenin-vladimir) was not there for February. He was in [Zurich](place:zurich), forty-six years old, reading about the fall of the Tsar in the Swiss papers several days late, and his first problem was geography: between him and Petrograd lay a war, and the shortest way home ran through the country Russia was fighting.

The German Foreign Ministry had spent two years looking for ways to take Russia out of the war from the inside, and a revolutionary who thought the war should be ended immediately was, from Berlin's point of view, a weapon that could be posted.[^merridale-2016] The arrangement they made was carefully hedged on both sides: a carriage with three of its four doors locked and a chalk line on the floor separating the Russians from the two German officers travelling with them, so that the party could say it had never legally entered Germany and the German government could say it had never legally admitted them. That is what "sealed train" means. Thirty-two people travelled in it.

They left Zurich on 9 April (N.S.), crossed at Gottmadingen that evening, ran the length of Germany, took the ferry from Sassnitz to Trelleborg, spent a day in Stockholm, went north to the head of the Gulf of Bothnia, crossed into Finland at Tornio, and came down to the [Finland Station](place:finland-station) late on the night of 16 April. In Petrograd it was 3 April. Both dates are correct; they are the same evening.

He arrived to a guard of honour of Kronstadt sailors, a band, searchlights and a welcoming speech from Chkheidze on behalf of the Petrograd Soviet, expressing the hope that the returning comrade would join in defending the revolution. Lenin turned away from him and addressed the crowd instead.[^figes-1996]

What he brought back was a position that nobody in his own party held. The Bolsheviks in Petrograd had spent March giving the Provisional Government conditional support and discussing reunion with the Mensheviks. The theses Lenin put to them the next day said the revolution was already passing into a second stage; that the government should have no support of any kind; that the war remained an imperialist war under a new flag; that there should be no parliamentary republic but a republic of Soviets; and that the party should change its name.[^figes-1996] They were voted down. *Pravda* printed them over his signature alone, with an editorial note saying they were his personal opinion.

Six weeks later they were the party's line, and by September they were the majority view in the capital's Soviet — not because Lenin had persuaded the leadership but because the war went on, the government went on promising to fight it, and the theses were the only programme on offer that took the promise back.[^rabinowitch-1976]

The German money is a separate question, and a real one. The train is not evidence of it: Berlin had every reason to help a man travel whether or not it was also paying him. What the German archives do and do not show is [its own argument](1917-russian-revolution:historiography-german-gold).
