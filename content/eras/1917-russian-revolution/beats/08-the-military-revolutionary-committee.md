Through September and early October [Lenin](person:lenin-vladimir), still in hiding in Finland, bombarded his Central Committee with letters saying that the time had come and that delay was criminal. The Central Committee, which had watched the party nearly destroyed in July, was not persuaded. He came back into the city in disguise on 10 October (O.S.) and argued through a night in a flat on the Karpovka until he carried the vote ten to two. The resolution put armed insurrection on the order of the day. It fixed no date, which is where the interesting part begins.[^rabinowitch-1976]

[Trotsky](person:trotsky-leon), who had been elected chairman of the Petrograd Soviet in September, wanted something different from what Lenin wanted. Lenin wanted the party to seize power, and soon, before the Congress of Soviets could dilute it. Trotsky wanted the Soviet to take power, at the Congress, so that what happened would look — and, on his argument, would *be* — the act of the elected representatives of the workers and soldiers rather than of a party.[^trotsky-1932] The distinction is tactical and it is also the whole question of what October was.

The instrument was to hand, and it had been created for something else. On the argument that the government meant to move the Petrograd garrison to the front — a plan the government really did have, and which the garrison really did not want — the Soviet set up a Military Revolutionary Committee to see that the capital was properly defended. It was not, on its face, an insurrectionary body. It was a defence committee of the elected Soviet, and men who would not have joined a Bolshevik conspiracy joined it without difficulty.[^rabinowitch-1976]

What it then did was administrative. It sent commissars to every unit and every arsenal in the city, and instructed them that no order of the district military command was valid without the committee's counter-signature. Over four days, unit by unit, the garrison agreed. On 21 October the district command refused to recognise the commissars; on 22 October the committee told the garrison that the district staff had put itself outside the revolution and was to be disregarded.

By the evening of 24 October (O.S.) the Provisional Government could not give an order to a single armed man in its own capital, and had not fought anybody for it. Everything that happened the following night was the consequence of a paperwork victory won in the week before.

That is why the argument about October is not really about the night of the 25th. It is about the fortnight that made the night a formality — and about whether a committee of an elected Soviet, doing what it did, was a revolution or [a coup wearing a Soviet's clothes](1917-russian-revolution:historiography-october-coup-or-revolution).
