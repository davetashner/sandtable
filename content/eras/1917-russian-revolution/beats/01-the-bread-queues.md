Russia in February 1917 was not starving. The 1916 harvest had been adequate and the empire had grain in it. What had broken was everything between the grain and the city: a railway network carrying an army of eleven million men, a currency the treasury was printing to pay for them, and a peasantry with steadily less reason to sell food for money that bought steadily less.[^stone-1975] Petrograd sat at the end of the longest supply line in Europe, its population swollen by war industry, and it was where the failure showed.

So there were queues. Women stood in them at night in a February that had just come out of a hard freeze, and the bakeries opened short. On 22 February (O.S.) the management of the [Putilov works](place:putilov-works) answered a wage dispute in the gun shop by locking out the whole plant, and put some thirty thousand men on the street with nothing to do.[^hasegawa-1981]

The next day was International Women's Day. The socialist parties had planned meetings and speeches; what they got was the women of the Vyborg textile mills walking out over bread and going into the metalworking plants to bring the men with them. By that evening something in the order of ninety thousand people were on strike, and the locked-out Putilov men were already outside to join them.[^hasegawa-1981]

Nobody had called it and nobody could stop it. On the 24th the crowds walked across the frozen Neva — the authorities were holding the bridges, which in a Petrograd February holds nothing — and were on the Nevsky Prospekt in the afternoon. On the 25th the city was shut. Trams stopped, newspapers did not appear, and the slogans on the banners went from *bread* to *down with the autocracy* in two days.[^figes-1996]

There is a temptation to read this backwards, as the first act of something. It was not experienced that way. There had been bread riots in Petrograd before, and strikes larger than this one; what nobody could yet know was that the government had already used up the last thing it had. It was going to try the garrison.
