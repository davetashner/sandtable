[Nicholas II](person:nicholas-ii) had been eight hundred kilometres of railway from his capital since 1915, at Stavka in [Mogilev](place:mogilev), having taken personal command of the army — a decision that left the government to the Empress and made every subsequent failure his.[^figes-1996] He left Mogilev before dawn on 28 February to go home to [Tsarskoe Selo](place:tsarskoe-selo), where his children had measles. The train got as far as Malaya Vishera in the small hours and was turned back: the line ahead was in revolutionary hands. It went back through Bologoye and round by Dno, and pulled into [Pskov](place:pskov), the headquarters of the Northern Front, on the evening of 1 March.

What ended three hundred and four years of Romanov rule was not a crowd. It was a set of telegrams. General Alekseev at Stavka polled the front and fleet commanders on whether the Emperor should abdicate, and one after another — Brusilov, Evert, Ruzsky, the Grand Duke Nikolai Nikolaevich, the fleets — replied that he should, because they could not answer for their own men if he did not.[^figes-1996] No army mutinied and nobody marched on Pskov. The generals looked at what the Petrograd garrison had done and drew a conclusion about their own.

He abdicated in the saloon car on 2 March. Then, told by the court physician that his haemophiliac son would not survive being separated from the family, he abdicated a second time a few hours later, this time for the boy as well, in favour of his brother Michael. The Fundamental Laws gave him no power to renounce the throne on his son's behalf, and that irregularity meant that the monarchy ended not with a settled succession but with a question nobody had any interest in raising.[^wade-2017]

The document is not called a manifesto by anybody who wrote it: its own title is *Akt* — an act — and it was never promulgated as a manifesto. It carries the time **15.05**. It was signed at about twenty to midnight, in front of Guchkov and Shulgin, and dated back to the hour the decision was taken. The instant on this pack's timeline is the one the document carries, and the discrepancy is left visible rather than resolved.

The next day Michael, in a flat in Petrograd, was told by almost everyone in the room that the capital would not accept him and that nobody could guarantee his safety. He declined the crown unless a Constituent Assembly should offer it. No Constituent Assembly ever did: the one elected in November 1917 met for a single day in January 1918 and was dispersed.

That is what "provisional" meant. From 3 March 1917 Russia was governed by men holding power on loan from an assembly that had not yet been elected, watched by a Soviet that could stop them at any moment, in the middle of a war they had promised to keep fighting.
