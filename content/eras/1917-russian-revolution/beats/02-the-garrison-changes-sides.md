On 26 February (O.S.) the government finally did what governments in that position do. Training detachments fired into the crowds on the Nevsky and on Znamenskaya Square and killed several dozen people, and the streets emptied. For one evening it looked as though the old arithmetic still worked.[^hasegawa-1981]

It did not survive the night. The men who had fired went back to barracks that were not barracks in any sense their officers would have recognised: reserve battalions of the Guards regiments, swollen to four and five thousand men each, packed into buildings meant for a fraction of that, filled with conscripts and returned wounded who had been told they were going to the front in the spring. They spent the night arguing about what they had done. In the morning the training detachment of the Volynsky Regiment refused to parade, shot the officer who insisted, and went out into the street to fetch the regiments next door.[^hasegawa-1981]

By the afternoon of 27 February the Preobrazhensky, the Litovsky, the Moscow and the Pavlovsky had gone the same way, the arsenal on the Liteiny had been broken open, the Kresty prison was empty, the district court was burning and something like forty thousand rifles were loose in the city. There was no plan and no leader. There was also, from that afternoon, no government: the ministers sat in the Mariinsky Palace and discovered that they could give orders to nobody at all.[^figes-1996]

This is the moment, and it is worth being exact about why. A crowd that is fired on is a demonstration. A crowd with the garrison inside it is a revolution — and the difference is not one of numbers or of will but of whether the men holding the rifles will use them. That question was decided in a barrack room on the Liteiny on the night of 26 February, by soldiers, about themselves.

That evening two bodies formed in two wings of the [Tauride Palace](place:tauride-palace). In one, the Duma's members — whose Duma the Emperor had prorogued the day before — constituted a Provisional Committee, because someone had to. In the other, socialists out of the prisons that morning and delegates elected in the factories and the barracks that afternoon constituted the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' Deputies, and within days added the soldiers.[^wade-2017] Neither had been elected to do what it was about to do. For the next eight months Russia had two governments in one building, connected by a corridor.
